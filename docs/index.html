Statistics

Everything below follows the filters above and the Latest price / Original ask toggle. Prices come from sale and trade ads that state a price. Variance and standard deviation (SD) are sample values (n−1). 95% intervals use the t distribution for means and the Wilson interval for percentages.

What moves the price

correlation with price
r is Pearson (linear), ρ is Spearman (rank). R² is the share of price variance a straight line on that variable explains. The slope is the average price change per unit, from a least-squares fit. Geekbench scores for about half the listings assume the chip's base configuration, which weakens those correlations.

Damage discount

vs undamaged median, same chip & RAM
Each damaged ad's price is compared with the median undamaged price for the same chip and RAM (needs 2+ undamaged ads). SD is in percentage points (pp).

Depreciation per year

undamaged sale ads · change in price per year of listing date
Fitted as log(price) against listing date, so the rate is a compound % per year. Raw uses the date alone. Spec-adjusted also holds RAM and storage constant, so a shift towards bigger configurations later in a chip's life doesn't hide its depreciation. The pooled row also holds the chip constant. Half-life is the years taken to lose half the value at the adjusted rate. Prices are nominal rand, so SA inflation (about 5% a year) offsets part of the real loss.
